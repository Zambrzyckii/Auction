# ADR-018: Scripted market bots

- **Status:** accepted (2026-11-18) · PL: [../../pl/adr/018-scripted-market-bots.md](../../pl/adr/018-scripted-market-bots.md)
- **Context:** the market had no counterparties. A listing got no bids unless a second player happened to be online, and a warehouse war cannot start below `MinPlayers` of 3 ([warehouses.md](../backend/warehouses.md)), so a single player could never see a war. The design note had promised Python "AI bots" listening to market events on RabbitMQ and acting through the gateway, and the backend side existed since the market feed of 2026-11-04 ([ADR-013](013-rabbitmq-transport.md)): a `Bot` role, API-key provisioning, long-lived tokens and the bot-only endpoints for funds and items. On 2026-11-18 the owner weighed three shapes. Agents driven by a language model would decide slowly, cost money per decision and could not be made to lose on purpose. One process per bot account would mean 100 processes, 100 queues and 100 address-sharing clients behind the gateway's 60 requests per second ([ADR-006](006-nginx-gateway.md)). One scripted fleet in a single process keeps the whole budget in one token bucket, lets the bots know each other and can be steered as one. Two constraints shaped it: the single `DeliveryProcessor` is the ceiling of every saga chain the fleet triggers ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md#known-limitations)), and nothing in `Backend/` or `Frontend/` changes for the bots.
- **Decision:** `Bots/` holds one asyncio process that provisions or logs in 100 bot accounts whose identities derive from the API key, so a rotated key means a new fleet. A `FeedConsumer` reads the four public auction events from its own durable queue on `auctionserver.market`, deduplicated on `MessageId`, into a `MarketState` that every agent shares and that resyncs through `GET /api/auctions` every 60 seconds. Each `BotAgent` is a persona sampled from six archetypes and decides through pure policy functions that take the market view, the persona, the wallet and a set of knobs and return a decision, with no IO and no clock inside. The policies carry the mercy rules: every account outside the fleet registry is a human, a fresh human listing at or below the official price is opened at the official price or one above the start, a bot never outbids a human past `buy_cap` of the official price, never in the last 40 seconds and yields with a probability that grows with the human's bids, and a human seller is valued with `sell_bonus`. A `Director` turns the median margin of the last 50 human outcomes into one `generosity` knob every minute, steers the listing rate to keep 20 to 30 fleet listings live and halves the saga rate when the bid saga's median latency exceeds 12 seconds. A `WarehouseWatcher` fills a lobby with a human to three players within seconds, keeps at most one bot-only war at a time and caps raises against a human at `buy_cap` of the decoded warehouse value. Every request goes through one `GatewayClient` with one token bucket at 45 requests per second and a burst of 60, a login gate of 1.2 seconds and a saga bucket of 0.5 per second for bids, listings and shop sales. The fleet runs as the Compose service `bots` behind the `bots` profile with its two secrets mounted as files, is verified by a virtual-time simulator with five guarantee scenarios and by the live probe `Bots/scripts/live_probe.py`, and is documented in [bots.md](../bots.md).
- **Consequences:** a player trading with the fleet almost always profits. A listing sells for at least the official price when any bot can afford it, a purchase from a bot costs at most `buy_cap` of the official price, 0.815 at the starting generosity of 0.3, and a human leading a war is never pushed past the cap. The fleet pays for this with money it mints for itself through `POST /api/wallets/funds`, so the economy gains funds at the pace of the top-ups. One process is one failure domain and one address, so the whole fleet shares one bucket under the gateway limit and a restart forgets the watcher's lobbies, while the accounts, wallets and items survive it. The fleet size is bounded by the saga bucket, not by the gateway: 100 bots place at most 30 bid sagas a minute between them, and a human's saga waits in the same `DeliveryProcessor` queue. The policies read their numbers from constants in the policy modules, so tuning them is a code change and an image rebuild, while the knobs move at run time. The decoded warehouse value exploits the fact that the estimate band reveals its variant ([warehouses.md](../backend/warehouses.md)), and the fleet uses that leak only to protect the human, never to win. A human listing above the official price gets a bid only from a bot whose boosted value exceeds its start.
